What the balance rests on

  • Lead in Ghana's own used batteries is the lead in new lead-acid batteries Ghana imported two to four years earlier. 2020 is left out because Ghana did not report it; partners' figures for that year are far above Ghana's own for other years. Batteries fitted to imported used vehicles are not counted.
  • Smelter intake is refined lead exports divided by recovery, which assumes Ghana uses almost no refined lead at home; any lead used at home would widen the gap.
  • The shortfall means unrecorded feed. Possible sources: spent batteries brought across land borders without being declared, batteries in imported used vehicles, or lead from other scrap. Côte d'Ivoire, for one, reported spent-battery shipments to Ghana in 2024 that Ghana did not record.
  • Informal breakers who sell plates or crude lead to exporting smelters end up inside the export figure, so the national balance cannot see informal work upstream of the smelters.

What the balance rests on

  • Lead in India's own used batteries is the battery share of India's refined lead consumption three years earlier, less the lead in that year's net exports of new batteries.
  • Registered intake is registered recyclers' share of India's recycled lead output (Frost & Sullivan), divided by recovery. For registered intake, the low case takes Autocar Pro's unsourced estimate of the informal share; the high case takes the environment ministry's figure for battery waste recycled through registered recyclers since August 2022, spread over the months since.
  • If registered plants also re-refine imported unwrought lead, their output overstates what they take from batteries and scrap, and the unaccounted share is higher than shown.
  • The official recycling figure covers all battery types and may include imported batteries, so it serves only as the high case for registered intake.

    How the balance works, and its sources

    For each country and year: lead available for recycling = lead in the country's own used batteries + lead in net imports of spent batteries + lead in net imports of lead scrap. Lead fed to industrial or registered smelters = their lead output ÷ recovery. The share unaccounted is what is left, as a share of the lead available. Where smelters need more lead than recorded sources supply, nothing is left unaccounted and the gap is unrecorded feed. Recycled and primary lead cannot be told apart in trade data, and lead used at home is not traded.

    Indonesia's balance is provisional: the lead in its own used batteries is Brian Wilson's 2021 estimate for Pure Earth, built from vehicles and batteries in use around 2020 and their service lives, and smelter feed is secondary lead output ÷ recovery, with USGS's 2022 estimate in the central case, 2024 refined lead exports as the low case and the formal sector's capacity, run full, as the high case.

    Nigeria, DR Congo and Ethiopia use Ghana's method: they make no batteries at home (assumed), so the lead in their own used batteries is the lead in the new batteries they imported two to four years earlier, net of exports, and smelter output is refined lead exports. Their panels set all the lead coming in (own used batteries and imports) against all the lead going out (smelter feed and exports of used batteries and scrap). In all five balances, spent-battery trade is the country's own report plus shipments that only its partners recorded, from UNCTAD's partner data; Ethiopia's exports use UNCTAD's estimate in place of a reported weight that is implausible for its value.

    Smelters are graded by the catalogue: A (field-confirmed) and B (active on two or more signals) count as confirmed; C is probably active; D is listed or stale; P is planned; X is closed or raided. Lead capacity is the catalogue's lead output figure for a plant or, where it gives only battery capacity, that capacity × 0.6; reported output is shown only where a company has published it.

    Trade-flow lines take each partner flow from the chosen country's own report where it gave a weight, and otherwise from the partner's report (dashed), using UNCTAD's partner data for spent lead-acid batteries (HS 8549.11) and new lead-acid batteries (HS 8507.10, 8507.20). Refined lead and scrap are not yet drawn by partner.

    For the 11 countries without a balance, secondary lead production is USGS's figure for 2022, and trade is each country's latest reported year (2024, or 2023 where 2024 is missing), from UN Comtrade and UNCTAD's battery trade monitor; partners' reports fill in where a country did not report.
